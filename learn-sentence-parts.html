By the end

What you’ll learn

  • Recognise a noun, a pronoun, and a verb.
  • Find the subject and direct object in a simple sentence.
  • Use endings as clues instead of relying only on word order.

Helpful first: Read your first words. These are suggestions; you can explore in any order.

Every example pairs Devanagari with IAST and an English meaning. Word boundaries stay visible; full external sandhi is postponed so you can focus on forms.

01

Begin with a small complete thought

A sentence tells us something. In these examples, a noun names a person and a verb tells what that person does. A noun can also name an object, a place, or an idea. A pronoun such as “I” or “she” can stand in for a noun. You will learn their forms later; for now, identify the job each word is doing.

Sanskrit has no separate articles corresponding exactly to English “a”, “an”, and “the”. Depending on context, bālaḥ can mean “a boy” or “the boy”. Our English translations choose whichever reads naturally. Do not search for a hidden Sanskrit word for every English article.

02

Ask three useful questions

First ask: what is happening? That locates the verb. Next ask: who or what is doing it? That locates the subject in our active examples. Finally ask whether the action directly affects someone or something. That may identify a direct object. Not every sentence needs one: “The boy plays” is already complete.

Learn a word together with its meaning, then use it in a small thought. Looking only at isolated vocabulary will not teach you how the words cooperate. Cover the translation of the book example and identify its three jobs before translating.

03

Endings carry information

English uses word order heavily to distinguish “the boy sees the girl” from “the girl sees the boy”. Sanskrit commonly marks roles through word endings. Changing an ending may change who acts or who receives the action. These changes are called inflection. Noun inflection is declension; verb inflection is conjugation.

A dictionary stem is the base used to organise a noun’s forms. बाल bāla is a stem; बालः bālaḥ is a form suitable for the subject of a singular active sentence. Do not assume that the stem by itself is always a complete sentence word. The next lessons explain which ending to choose.

Keep in mind

Examples in this course keep words separate and sometimes retain their pause forms. Sound changes between words, called external sandhi, are intentionally postponed. This lets you see each grammatical form clearly.

04

Build one sentence, then change one part

A useful beginner order is subject → object, if needed → verb. Sanskrit word order is flexible, but this pattern keeps practice manageable. Start with “The girl reads a book”. Change “reads” to “writes” and you have a new sentence while retaining the same broad structure. Change only the subject and check that the verb still agrees with it.

For now, use the supplied singular forms rather than inventing endings. In the final lesson you will construct sentences independently. Before moving on, explain in your own words why “book” is an object in the reading sentence, while “boy” is a subject in the playing sentence.

Common mistakes

  • Do not equate the first word with the subject in every Sanskrit sentence.
  • A dictionary stem and a complete inflected word are not always identical.
  • Not every verb needs a direct object.

1. In बालिका पुस्तकं पठति। — bālikā pustakaṃ paṭhati, what is being read?

2. Which word is a verb?

3. Which beginner word order will we usually practise?

With JavaScript off, try each question, then open its answer.

In बालिका पुस्तकं पठति। — bālikā pustakaṃ paṭhati, what is being read?
  • The girl
  • The book
  • The verb ending

Answer: The book Pustakaṃ is the book, the direct object of reading.

Which word is a verb?
  • पुस्तकम् — pustakam
  • बालिका — bālikā
  • लिखति — likhati

Answer: लिखति — likhati Likhati means “writes” or “is writing”.

Which beginner word order will we usually practise?
  • Subject → object → verb
  • Always verb → subject → object
  • Alphabetical order

Answer: Subject → object → verb Subject–object–verb is a useful practice pattern, though actual Sanskrit word order is flexible.

Make it your own

Practise on your own

Write an answer or say it aloud before opening the solution.

1. Identify the subject and verb in बालः क्रीडति। — bālaḥ krīḍati.

Reveal solution 1
Subject: बालः bālaḥ — the boy. Verb: क्रीडति krīḍati — plays. There is no direct object.

2. Replace “reads” with “writes” in “The girl reads a book”.

Reveal solution 2
बालिका पुस्तकं लिखति। bālikā pustakaṃ likhati. — The girl writes a book. The action changes; the subject and object remain.

Lesson recap

  • Find the action, then its subject and any object.
  • Nouns name; verbs tell actions or states; pronouns stand in for nouns.
  • Endings help identify grammatical roles.
