01

A familiar stem ending, a different gender

The neuter stem फल phala — fruit ends in short a, just like masculine bāla. Its gender tells you to choose a different pattern for the nominative and accusative. The singular form is फलम् phalam whether the fruit is the subject or direct object.

This gives you a particularly useful general rule: neuter nominative and accusative forms are identical within each number. The rule applies beyond this one stem type, although the actual endings differ among declensions. Learn both the rule and the specific forms; neither replaces the other.

02

One fruit, two fruits, many fruits

Neuter phala: subject and direct object
CaseOneTwoMany
Nominativeफलम् phalamफले phaleफलानि phalāni
Accusativeफलम् phalamफले phaleफलानि phalāni

Notice the long ā in phalāni. The dual phale is exactly two, not a vague plural. The same form phale can also be locative singular, as you will see below. Forms that coincide must be interpreted through context.

Phalam appears as phalaṃ before a consonant in these sentences. The nasal spelling does not by itself distinguish subject from object. Ask what the verb means and which participant does the action.

03

The remaining singular cases

Phala: singular forms beyond the subject and object
CaseFormTypical sense
Instrumentalफलेन phalenaby / with a fruit
Dativeफलाय phalāyafor a fruit
Ablativeफलात् phalātfrom a fruit
Genitiveफलस्य phalasyaof a fruit
Locativeफले phalein / on a fruit
Vocativeफल phalaO fruit!

These follow the same broad singular pattern as masculine a-stems outside the nominative and accusative. A vocative addressed to a fruit is unusual in everyday conversation, but the table shows the grammatical form. A full paradigm contains forms whether or not every one is equally common in daily speech.

Compare phale in two contexts: when paired with a dual verb meaning “fall”, it can be the nominative dual, “two fruits”. In a phrase describing something inside a fruit, it can be locative singular. No single English translation belongs permanently to those letters.

04

Use the model with books

पुस्तक pustaka — book is another neuter a-stem. Its subject and object forms are pustakam, pustake, pustakāni. Practise changing the number of books while holding the reader constant.

End by covering the table and saying the three book forms. Then explain why paṭhati stays the same in all three sentences. Understanding the subject–object distinction makes the identical neuter endings much less confusing.

Common mistakes

  • An -am form is not always an object; it can be a neuter singular subject.
  • Phale can represent more than one case-and-number combination.
  • Preserve the long ā in phalāni and pustakāni.

Check your understanding

Choose one answer for each question. You can retry as often as you like.

1. What are “fruits” in the nominative plural?

2. In फलं पतति। — phalaṃ patati (“A fruit falls”), what is phalaṃ?

3. Which pair of cases has matching neuter forms in every number?

With JavaScript off, try each question, then open its answer.

What are “fruits” in the nominative plural?
  • फलाः — phalāḥ
  • फलानि — phalāni
  • फले — phale

Answer: फलानि — phalāni Neuter a-stems use -āni in the nominative and accusative plural.

In फलं पतति। — phalaṃ patati (“A fruit falls”), what is phalaṃ?
  • The subject
  • An instrument
  • A direct object

Answer: The subject The fruit is what falls; its form can be nominative despite the ending -am / -aṃ.

Which pair of cases has matching neuter forms in every number?
  • Genitive and locative
  • Dative and nominative
  • Nominative and accusative

Answer: Nominative and accusative This is the central neuter pattern; the actual endings depend on the stem class.

Make it your own

Practise on your own

Write an answer or say it aloud before opening the solution.

1. Change “The girl reads a book” to “The girl reads two books”.

Reveal solution 1
बालिका पुस्तके पठति। bālikā pustake paṭhati. — The girl reads two books. Pustake is dual; paṭhati remains singular.

2. Why can phalam be a subject in one sentence and an object in another?

Reveal solution 2
Neuter nominative and accusative are identical. The verb and the relationships in the sentence distinguish the two uses.
