What you’ll learn

  • Recognise short and long vowels.
  • Distinguish dental, retroflex, and aspirated consonants.
  • Use IAST as a guide while learning the script.

No prior Sanskrit knowledge needed. Take your time and read the examples aloud.

Every example pairs Devanagari with IAST and an English meaning. Word boundaries stay visible; full external sandhi is postponed so you can focus on forms.

01

Start with sounds, not English spelling

Sanskrit spelling makes distinctions that English spelling often hides. A long vowel and a short vowel are different sounds; a consonant with a puff of breath and one without it are different consonants. You do not need to memorise the whole alphabet today. Begin by noticing these contrasts and reading the examples slowly.

Every lesson gives the Sanskrit script, a matching IAST transliteration, and an English meaning. IAST uses Roman letters with marks: a line above a vowel indicates length, and dots below certain letters distinguish sounds. Transliteration records the Sanskrit spelling; it is not an English translation or a perfect substitute for hearing a teacher.

02

Give long vowels more time

Practise a short vowel for one beat and its long partner for approximately two. Keep the vowel steady rather than turning it into an English glide. The first three pairs are the most useful place to begin.

Three everyday short–long pairs

अ aआ āLength
इ iई īLength
उ uऊ ūLength

The wider inventory includes ऋ ṛ and its rare long partner ॠ ṝ, the rare ऌ ḷ, and ए ऐ ओ औ e ai o au. The last four count as long vowels. Syllabic ṛ is itself a vowel; do not routinely insert a full i after r. Learn the common six vowels first, then recognise the others as they occur.

03

The consonants have an orderly map

The five stop-consonant rows move from the back of the mouth towards the lips. Within each row, the second and fourth sounds are aspirated: they include a release of breath. For example, kh is one aspirated consonant, not the two syllables ka-ha. The dental t touches the teeth; retroflex ṭ uses the tongue turned back towards the roof of the mouth.

Five rows of consonants (with inherent a for practice)

Palateच छ ज झ ञca cha ja jha ña

The remaining consonants are य र ल व ya ra la va, श ष स śa ṣa sa, and ह ha. In IAST, c is a single consonant like the opening sound of “church”; th is an aspirated t, not English “th” in “think”. Text descriptions are approximate: a teacher can help refine tongue position.

04

Two signs you will meet immediately

ं ṃ — anusvāra marks a nasal sound whose realisation depends on its context. ः ḥ — visarga marks a breath sound after a vowel. Neither is simply decorative punctuation. A full lesson on how sounds change between words comes later, outside this foundation tree.

For a two-minute practice, read the three short–long pairs aloud three times. Then alternate ka and kha, ta and ṭa. Finish by identifying the long vowels in bālaḥ and mālā. Success here means noticing the distinctions, not producing every sound perfectly on the first attempt.

Common mistakes

  • Do not pronounce ā, ī, and ū as ordinary short vowels.
  • Do not read th as English “th” or treat ḥ as punctuation.
Pause & check

Check your understanding

Choose one answer for each question. You can retry as often as you like.

1. Which vowel is long?

2. How is ख — kha different from क — ka?

3. What does the dot in ṭ help distinguish?

With JavaScript off, try each question, then open its answer.

Which vowel is long?
  • इ — i
  • ई — ī
  • अ — a

Answer: ई — ī The macron in ī marks a long vowel; इ i is its short partner.

How is ख — kha different from क — ka?
  • It is aspirated, with a puff of breath
  • It is a long vowel
  • It has no vowel

Answer: It is aspirated, with a puff of breath The kh consonant is aspirated. Both written forms here include a.

What does the dot in ṭ help distinguish?
  • Plural number
  • A long vowel
  • A retroflex consonant

Answer: A retroflex consonant IAST ṭ is retroflex; t without the dot is dental.

Make it your own

Practise on your own

Write an answer or say it aloud before opening the solution.

1. Write the long partners of अ a, इ i, and उ u.

Reveal solution 1
आ ई ऊ ā ī ū. Hold each vowel longer.

2. Read बालः bālaḥ. Identify the long vowel and final sign.

Reveal solution 2
The long vowel is आ ā. The final sign is ः ḥ — visarga. The word means “boy”.
