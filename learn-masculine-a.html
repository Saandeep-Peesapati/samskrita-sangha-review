What you’ll learn

  • Distinguish a short-a stem from its sentence forms.
  • Use the eight singular forms of bāla.
  • Recognise subject and object forms in all three numbers.

Helpful first: The eight case roles. These are suggestions; you can explore in any order.

Every example pairs Devanagari with IAST and an English meaning. Word boundaries stay visible; full external sandhi is postponed so you can focus on forms.

01

Choose a model before choosing an ending

A declension pattern is a family of forms used by nouns with a particular kind of stem. Here our model is masculine बाल bāla — boy. The final short a belongs to the stem. The singular subject is bālaḥ, while the singular object is bālam. A noun is still the same noun when its form changes; the form tells you how it works in a sentence.

Other useful masculine a-stems include देव deva — god, ग्राम grāma — village, and वृक्ष vṛkṣa — tree. This lesson covers the singular case set plus the subject and object forms for two and many. You can begin forming sentences without learning every possible noun paradigm at once.

02

Eight singular forms, eight jobs

Masculine bāla: singular
CaseFormTypical sense
Nominativeबालः bālaḥthe boy as subject
Accusativeबालम् bālamthe boy as object
Instrumentalबालेन bālenaby / with the boy
Dativeबालाय bālāyato / for the boy
Ablativeबालात् bālātfrom the boy
Genitiveबालस्य bālasyaof the boy
Locativeबाले bālein / on the boy
Vocativeबाल bālaO boy!

Treat each row as a meaningful unit. To express possession, say bālasya and “of the boy” together. To address the boy, use bāla rather than the subject form bālaḥ. Repeating forms with their jobs is more effective than learning eight sounds without context.

The visible result is not always made by simply adding letters after the dictionary form. The final vowel can change or combine with an ending: bāla becomes bāle in the locative. Learn the finished model forms first; detailed sound rules can be studied after the foundation course.

03

Subject and object: one, two, many

The most useful number contrast
RoleOneTwoMany
Subjectबालः bālaḥबालौ bālauबालाः bālāḥ
Objectबालम् bālamबालौ bālauबालान् bālān

In the dual, the nominative and accusative coincide: bālau can be subject or object. In the plural, bālāḥ and bālān are different. Long ā occurs in both of those plural forms, so retaining vowel length matters.

04

Transfer the pattern carefully

There are sound adjustments in some words even within a broad pattern. For example, the instrumental of राम rāma is रामेण rāmeṇa, with retroflex ṇ, whereas bālena has n. Do not use a substitution rule so mechanically that it erases genuine sound changes.

Practise with grāma: choose the form for “of the village”, then “in the village”, then “from the village”. Say why you chose each case before consulting the table. Finally return to bāla and make one sentence with it as subject and one with it as object. The point is to choose forms for a purpose.

Common mistakes

  • The stem bāla is not the ordinary singular subject bālaḥ.
  • Bālāḥ is a plural subject; bālān is a plural object.
  • Some stems trigger sound changes: rāmeṇa has ṇ.
Pause & check

Check your understanding

Choose one answer for each question. You can retry as often as you like.

1. Choose “of the boy”.

2. Which form means “the boys” as the direct object?

3. Choose “in the village”.

With JavaScript off, try each question, then open its answer.

Choose “of the boy”.
  • बालम् — bālam
  • बालस्य — bālasya
  • बालाय — bālāya

Answer: बालस्य — bālasya Bālasya is genitive singular. Bālam is accusative; bālāya is dative.

Which form means “the boys” as the direct object?
  • बालान् — bālān
  • बालाः — bālāḥ
  • बालः — bālaḥ

Answer: बालान् — bālān The masculine a-stem accusative plural ends in -ān.

Choose “in the village”.
  • ग्रामस्य — grāmasya
  • ग्रामात् — grāmāt
  • ग्रामे — grāme

Answer: ग्रामे — grāme Grāme is locative singular; the other forms mean “of” and “from” the village.

Make it your own

Practise on your own

Write an answer or say it aloud before opening the solution.

1. Give the singular forms “of the village”, “in the village”, and “from the village”.

Reveal solution 1
ग्रामस्य grāmasya — of the village; ग्रामे grāme — in the village; ग्रामात् grāmāt — from the village.

2. Why does बालिका बालान् पश्यति। — bālikā bālān paśyati use a singular verb?

Reveal solution 2
It means “The girl sees the boys”. Bālikā, the subject, is singular. Bālān is a plural direct object and does not determine the verb’s number.
