What you’ll learn

  • Break a short written word into sound units.

Every example pairs Devanagari with IAST and an English meaning. Word boundaries stay visible; full external sandhi is postponed so you can focus on forms.

01

A consonant normally includes a

Devanagari is built around syllables. The ordinary consonant letter क ka includes the vowel a unless another sign changes it. To write just the consonant k, use क् k. The small mark underneath is the virāma: it cancels the inherent vowel. This is why reading Sanskrit as a collection of English-style letters can produce extra or missing vowels.

An independent vowel letter is used when a vowel begins a syllable without a preceding consonant. A vowel following a consonant usually appears as a dependent mark, often called a mātrā. These are two ways to represent a vowel, not two different vowel sounds.

One consonant, several vowel marks

In कि ki, the i mark is printed to the left of the consonant, but you still pronounce k before i. In कु ku and कू kū, look below the consonant. When handwriting, pay attention to which consonant each mark belongs to; the horizontal headline can make several syllables look like one unit.

The same principle works with other consonants: प पा पि पी पु पू pa pā pi pī pu pū. Read each series aloud, then cover the Roman letters and try again.

03

Join consonants without inserting vowels

Two adjacent consonants without a vowel between them form a cluster. They may appear as a combined shape or as a reduced letter joined to the next. For instance, क्त kta combines k and t before a; it does not mean kata. In पुस्तकम् pustakam — book, the cluster st occurs between pu and ka.

At a word’s end, म् m is a consonant without a vowel. You will also meet a dot above the line, ं ṃ, especially before another consonant. This course shows isolated noun forms with final m and often uses ṃ before consonants in sentences. The transliteration always matches the displayed spelling.

04

A repeatable reading routine

First find the vowels and vowel marks. Next check for a virāma or joined consonants. Then read from left to right in syllables. Finally compare the IAST and the meaning. This is a reading routine, not a demand to understand every grammatical ending immediately.

The sign । daṇḍa closes a sentence or a verse segment. It is not spoken as a sound. Read अहं पठामि। ahaṃ paṭhāmi. — I read. slowly: a-haṃ, pa-ṭhā-mi. Notice both the nasal sign and the long ā. Spend a minute copying the sentence, then point to each mark and say what it does.

Common mistakes

  • A mark printed before a consonant need not be pronounced first: कि is ki.
  • Final म् is m, not ma.
  • Do not add vowels inside consonant clusters.
Pause & check

Check your understanding

Choose one answer for each question. You can retry as often as you like.

1. How do you read कि?

2. What does the virāma in क् do?

3. Which spelling is kā?

With JavaScript off, try each question, then open its answer.

How do you read कि?
  • ik
  • ki
  • kī

Answer: ki The i mark is written on the left but pronounced after k.

What does the virāma in क् do?
  • Adds a long vowel
  • Makes the word plural
  • Removes the inherent a

Answer: Removes the inherent a क् represents k without a following vowel.

Which spelling is kā?
  • का
  • क
  • कि

Answer: का The ा sign supplies long ā.

Make it your own

Practise on your own

Write an answer or say it aloud before opening the solution.

1. Write ka, ki, and kū in Devanagari.

Reveal solution 1
क कि कू ka ki kū. The marks distinguish a, i, and long ū.

2. Why does फलम् phalam not end in ma?

Reveal solution 2
The virāma under the final म् m removes a. फलम् phalam means “fruit”.

Lesson recap

  • A plain consonant includes a.
  • A vowel mark changes that vowel; virāma removes it.
  • Read clusters and final consonants carefully.
