What you’ll learn

  • Recognise the long-ā feminine noun pattern.
  • Use bālikā in singular case roles.
  • Compare feminine subject and object forms across number.

Helpful first: The eight case roles. These are suggestions; you can explore in any order.

Every example pairs Devanagari with IAST and an English meaning. Word boundaries stay visible; full external sandhi is postponed so you can focus on forms.

01

Keep the final vowel long

Our feminine model is बालिका bālikā — girl. Its stem ends in long ā, and its singular nominative looks exactly like that stem. This differs from masculine bāla, whose nominative is bālaḥ. Do not add a visarga to every noun that is the subject of a sentence.

Other regular feminine ā-stems include लता latā — creeper and माला mālā — garland. Feminine nouns also have other stem types, such as nadī, “river”. Today we learn one productive pattern, not all feminine declensions.

02

The singular pattern

Bālikā: singular
CaseFormTypical sense
Nominativeबालिका bālikāthe girl as subject
Accusativeबालिकाम् bālikāmthe girl as object
Instrumentalबालिकया bālikayāby / with the girl
Dativeबालिकायै bālikāyaito / for the girl
Ablativeबालिकायाः bālikāyāḥfrom the girl
Genitiveबालिकायाः bālikāyāḥof the girl
Locativeबालिकायाम् bālikāyāmin / on the girl
Vocativeबालिके bālikeO girl!

The instrumental bālikayā has short a before yā; compare the long ā in bālikāyai and bālikāyāḥ. Read the transliteration carefully instead of treating the last several syllables as one blur. The ablative and genitive share a form, so their meaning depends on context.

Some literal case translations are more natural with another noun. Latāyām, “on the creeper”, is an ordinary locative phrase. Bālikāyām illustrates the form, even though its use needs an appropriate context. A paradigm teaches possibilities; a sentence supplies a situation.

03

Compare the three numbers

Feminine ā-stem subject and object forms
RoleOneTwoMany
Subjectबालिका bālikāबालिके bālikeबालिकाः bālikāḥ
Objectबालिकाम् bālikāmबालिके bālikeबालिकाः bālikāḥ

The dual and plural subject/object forms coincide in this pattern, while the singular distinguishes bālikā from bālikām. Bālike is also the singular vocative, “O girl!” Once again a form needs context. The reader cannot decide solely from its final letter.

04

Transfer the pattern and explain the choice

To build “of the garland”, first identify possession, then choose genitive singular, then use the mālā pattern to obtain mālāyāḥ. This order prevents the common mistake of choosing an ending before deciding what it needs to express.

Practise with a sheet of paper: put bālikā, bālikām, and bālikayā in one column; write “subject”, “object”, and “by/with” in another. Match them without looking at the table. Then produce the same three forms for mālā. Finish by saying why the noun’s feminine gender does not change the present-tense verb paṭhati.

Common mistakes

  • Do not add ḥ to singular subject bālikā.
  • Distinguish bālikayā from bālikāyai.
  • Do not apply the ā-stem table to every feminine noun.
Pause & check

Check your understanding

Choose one answer for each question. You can retry as often as you like.

1. Choose “of the girl”.

2. Which is the singular subject form?

3. How do you say “by / with the garland”?

With JavaScript off, try each question, then open its answer.

Choose “of the girl”.
  • बालिकया — bālikayā
  • बालिकाम् — bālikām
  • बालिकायाः — bālikāyāḥ

Answer: बालिकायाः — bālikāyāḥ Bālikāyāḥ is genitive singular; it can also be ablative in another context.

Which is the singular subject form?
  • बालिका — bālikā
  • बालिकाः — bālikāḥ
  • बालिकाम् — bālikām

Answer: बालिका — bālikā Bālikā is nominative singular. The visarga form bālikāḥ is plural here.

How do you say “by / with the garland”?
  • मालायै — mālāyai
  • मालया — mālayā
  • मालाम् — mālām

Answer: मालया — mālayā Mālayā follows the instrumental pattern bālikayā.

Make it your own

Practise on your own

Write an answer or say it aloud before opening the solution.

1. Give the object forms for one, two, and many girls.

Reveal solution 1
बालिकाम् बालिके बालिकाः bālikām bālike bālikāḥ. These are accusative singular, dual, and plural.

2. Give “of the creeper” and “on the creeper”.

Reveal solution 2
लतायाः latāyāḥ — of the creeper; लतायाम् latāyām — on the creeper. Choose genitive for possession and locative for location.
Take this with you

Lesson recap

  • The model’s stem and singular nominative are bālikā.
  • Bālikām is singular accusative; bālikayā is instrumental.
  • Several forms coincide, so read them in context.
